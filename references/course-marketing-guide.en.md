# Course Marketing usage guide

[Interactive navigator](https://yaojingang.github.io/yao-open-prompts/course-marketing.html?lang=en) · [English directory](../prompts-en/08-ai-marketing/course-marketing/README.md) · [Chinese guide](course-marketing-guide.md)

These 17 companion prompts by Yao Jingang support teachers, knowledge creators, and education product teams. They cover positioning, design, sales, and operations while retaining the book's chapter numbering.

## Start with one task

1. Choose the chapter matching your current task. For a first course, follow positioning, design, sales, then operations.
2. Copy the `Prompt` body or open it in the navigator. Replace examples and placeholders in braces with real information, including course names in output headings.
3. Paste it into your preferred conversational model. Answer follow-up questions and continue the conversation.
4. Select a proposal, ask for specific refinements, and validate it through learner interviews and practical results.

The navigator supports search, stage filters, direct editing, copy, and download. Save `course-marketing.html` and open it locally for offline use. Edits remain in the current page session.

## Prepare a course brief

```text
My expertise and experience:
Target audience and one specific learner:
The learner's most urgent problem:
Course topic and measurable learning goals:
Existing concepts and materials:
Delivery format, service period, and available time:
Channels, competitor observations, and prices:
Existing cases and their sources:
```

Use the relevant parts in each template. Confirmed conclusions from one template can become inputs for the next.

## Validate inputs and outputs

- The mission template asks for simulated answers. For real positioning work, append: Ask your questions first, then wait for my real answers before generating proposals.
- Replace the sample courses and output examples too. Work on one course at a time, and one lesson at a time for teaching design.
- Validate market gaps, competitor prices, and learner needs with research. Match outcome claims, testimonials, credentials, refunds, and service promises to actual evidence and capacity.
- Numerical multipliers in the Chinese chapter titles come from the book. The prompts provide planning ideas; outcomes depend on the course, learners, and resources.

## Source handling

The supplied manuscript contains 18 chapter entries. Chapter 3.5, about launch and brand momentum, repeats chapter 2.6's delivery prompt and usage instructions exactly. Deduplication yields 17 templates: four positioning, six design, four sales, and three operations.

When reading chapter 3.5, refer to [2.6 Delivery](../prompts-en/08-ai-marketing/course-marketing/course-delivery-value.md). A distinct chapter 3.5 prompt awaits author input. Chinese prompt blocks preserve the source verbatim. English files translate the roles, tasks, examples, and output structures. The [Chinese guide](course-marketing-guide.md) also retains the manuscript's original usage instructions.

## Suggested follow-up questions

- 1.1: Ask me the questions first, then wait for my real answers before creating the mission statements.
- 1.2: Write three introductions based on my chosen persona.
- 1.3: List the interview questions and real data needed to validate this opportunity.
- 1.4: Turn the core pain point into five learner interview questions.
- 2.1: Explain which audience and channel suit each of these three titles.
- 2.2: Add a learning objective and assessable assignment to each module.
- 2.3: Schedule each activity within a 45-minute lesson.
- 2.4: Prioritize traceable real examples and mark information that needs verification.
- 2.5: Test whether the framework explains a concrete case completely.
- 2.6: Rank the ideas by implementation cost and learner benefit, then choose three priorities.
- 3.1: List the pricing assumptions and the data required to validate them separately.
- 3.2: Break the first month into weekly actions within my time budget.
- 3.3: Check that outcome claims, refund commitments, and endorsements are supported.
- 3.4: Write ready-to-post social and community copy for day one.
- 4.1: Write the opening five-minute script and interaction questions.
- 4.2: Add a Q&A schedule, service boundaries, and a weekly review sheet.
- 4.3: Choose one advanced product to validate first based on real learner needs.
