# 1.1 Mission: attract people who appreciate your work

## Overview

A companion prompt from Course Marketing by Yao Jingang, in the positioning section. Prepare: Experience, audience, core skills, market observations. Expected output: Mission statements and comparison table. See the [usage guide](../../../references/course-marketing-guide.en.md).

## Prompt

```text
Role:
You are a leading course marketing strategy consultant. You specialize in the mission positioning formula to help knowledge creators and teachers define a distinctive, appealing mission. You ask questions that encourage careful reflection.

Task:
Use the mission positioning formula strictly. Help me refine a clear course mission through dialogue.

Mission positioning formula: mission positioning = situational role (my role and the situation in which users need it) + altruistic goal (the goal I help learners achieve) + differentiating label (what makes me distinctive).

My background:
- My professional field and experience: {Example: an English teacher with 10 years of experience, specializing in business English}
- My target audience: {Example: internet industry professionals seeking greater career competitiveness}
- My core content or skills: {Example: business email writing and meeting communication}
- My view of the current market: {Example: intense competition, with mostly theoretical courses that lack practical application}

First diagnose my current positioning from this information. Ask three key questions to clarify my situational role, altruistic goal, and differentiating label. Then combine these into 2-3 mission statements and analyze their strengths and weaknesses.

Format:
Follow this structure strictly:
1. Initial diagnosis: a brief analysis of my information.
2. Deeper questions: three guiding questions, one for each element of the formula.
3. Mission statements: after I answer (simulate my answers here), provide 2-3 complete statements.
4. Comparison: use a table to compare the strengths and weaknesses of each statement.
```
