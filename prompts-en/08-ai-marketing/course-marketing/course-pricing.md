# 3.1 Price: build a value-based pricing strategy

## Overview

A companion prompt from Course Marketing by Yao Jingang, in the sales section. Prepare: Topic, audience, value, competitor prices, brand positioning. Expected output: Base-price analysis, packages, early-bird strategy. See the [usage guide](../../../references/course-marketing-guide.en.md).

## Prompt

```text
Role:
You are a marketing strategist and course pricing expert. You understand that price is a marketing tool. Combine pricing principles and psychology to create a knowledge-product price system that communicates value and supports sales.

Task:
Design a complete pricing and sales strategy using the two-step pricing method below.

My course information:
- Topic: {Example: Python data analysis for beginners}
- Audience: {Example: professionals changing careers or improving data skills}
- Core problem solved or value created: {Example: develop skills for data analysis roles and improve career competitiveness}
- Competitor price ranges: {Example: 300-800 yuan for introductory courses, 1,500-4,000 yuan for advanced project-based courses}
- Desired brand positioning: {Example: premium, practical training that helps learners find work}

Format:
### Pricing strategy proposal for {course topic}
Step 1. Establish a reasonable base price
1. Competitor perspective: [Reasonable range based on the provided prices]
2. Learner value: [Consider the value created, such as potential salary growth]
3. Brand strategy: [Price implications of the intended positioning]
4. Base-price recommendation: [A specific recommended price]

Step 2. Build price competitiveness with psychological strategies
1. High-price anchor: [Design a higher-priced offer, such as one-to-one consulting]
2. Middle option: [Design standard, pro, and premium packages, identifying the main offer]
3. Limited-time offer and loss aversion: [Design an appealing early-bird offer]

Overall launch recommendation:
[A final, clear launch pricing strategy]
```
